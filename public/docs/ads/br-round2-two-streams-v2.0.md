# Round 2 · Two cold streams

**v1.9 · 14 August 2026.** For the ad restart on **Monday 1 September**. Round 1 is archived.

---

## Why Round 1 is archived rather than continued

Round 1 was built four-patterns-equal, before we had looked properly at who is actually in the
database. Three problems, none of them about the craft:

1. **Insulin drift was one of the four live ads.** Insulin is **one in twenty-five** of the
   profiles we have run. A quarter of the spend was aimed at four per cent of the audience.
2. **Nothing in the pack was about sleep or stress load** — the two worst-scoring sections we
   measure, and now the biggest lane.
3. **Everything routed to `/challenge`.** Fourteen days is a large first ask of a stranger.

The formula is unchanged and still correct: **pattern interrupt + burning intrigue + specific
benefit**. Only the aim has moved.

---

## The destination · the Body Decode LP

**Both streams land on `/decode`.** Updated 24 Aug 2026 at cutover; `/challenge` now redirects there, but the ads should point at `/decode` directly so the pixel is not learning on a redirect.

> ad click → **Challenge landing page** → sign up → welcome email with portal access →
> **the scorecard is the first thing inside the portal**

So the scorecard **is** the first gate. It sits after the signup rather than in front of it, and
that order is deliberate: name, email and phone are captured **before** any gate, so someone who
stalls at the scorecard is still a lead we can chase. On a scorecard-first route they would leave
nothing behind, because the public scorecard only asks for contact details after all five
sections are answered.

The public `/scorecard` page stays the **warm and organic** front door. Cold paid and the
gym-floor QR go direct to the Challenge, per the locked routing rule.

## The creative system · unchanged from Round 1

The visual system **is** the high-dopamine strategy, not decoration around it. Round 2 wears the
same clothes; only the aim moved. Every ad carries all seven parts:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Photo** | A real greyscale human in a real gym, so the frame does not read as an ad in the half-second before anyone decides. |
| **Artifact** | A product screen floating over the photo. **The highest-value element in the system** — it is proof, it is unfamiliar, and it is the thing the eye stops on. A different one in every ad. |
| **Tags** | Blue + white pair, lower left. |
| **Rule** | 4px blue, separating image from argument. |
| **Strap** | Headline with exactly **one** blue emphasis phrase, plus a grey subline. |
| **Ticker** | Blue marquee. |
| **Offer bar** | Free / no payment, so the price objection dies inside the image. |

Two arrangements, both carried over: **NEWS** (full-bleed photo, artifact upper right, strap
beneath) and **SPLIT** (blue offer banner on top, white product card left, photo and headline
right). A1 and B1 are SPLIT; the rest are NEWS.

> **Why the sub changed, and it was not just the name.** It used to promise *"Training plan,
> nutrition guide, daily coaching"*. None of those are in the free tier any more; they moved into
> the $97 Blueprint at cutover. That is not a stale product name, it is a promise the product does
> not keep, and anyone clicking it would have arrived expecting a plan and been handed a read.

**The offer bar CHANGED at cutover, 24 Aug 2026.** It now reads *Free · The Body Decode*.
"Decode" is still the locked product term for paid cold ads; "readiness" is the category the tag
pair carries.

**The offer zone carries the benefit, not just the product name.** It used to read *Free ·
14-Day Body Decode Challenge* over *Find your pattern in 14 days*. To a stranger that is two
phrases they have never heard and a price. Pattern interrupt and burning intrigue were both
strong; **the third leg of the formula — a specific benefit — was carried by nothing**, and that
is also the leg that lets the algorithm find the right people.

It now reads: *Two minutes of questions. Then your report.* Set in sentence case, not
tracked-out caps, because it is the one part of the image
meant to be **read** rather than scanned.

**This costs no dopamine, because intrigue and clarity live in different zones.** The strap
carries the curiosity gap, the offer bar carries what you get. Making the bar plainer never
touches the hook. The ticker stays pure brand on purpose: a news ticker repeating a name is what
makes the frame read as a broadcast rather than an ad, and filling it with offer language would
undo the interrupt the whole system exists to create.

**SPLIT layout rebalanced (A1, B1).** It centred a ~730px content block in a ~1250px frame, so
roughly 190px of dead black sat above *and* below and the composition had nothing anchoring it.
Fixed without inventing content: the photo is much taller (a bigger photo is a better pattern
interrupt anyway), the card rows breathe, and the card gained a footer explaining what the
numbers mean — a stranger previously saw *1.80 /3* with no idea what good looks like. The score
disc was also using `align-items:baseline`, which does no vertical centring at all, so the number
sat high in its ring.

**Type rebuilt to the mobile floor (v1.9).** A 4:5 post renders about 430px wide on a phone
against our 1080px canvas, so everything scales by roughly **0.4**. The artifact panel was
running **11 to 16px** — four to six pixels on a phone. The proof element of the entire system,
and nobody could read a word of it. **29 of 37 type declarations were under the floor.**

Everything is now at or above **30px**, per the locked scale: body 39, list rows 34-36, captions
and notes 30-32, kickers 32, footer benefit 34. The panel had to grow from 344px to 660px to
hold it, so it covers more of the photo. That is the right trade — the artifact is the thing the
eye stops on.

**The SPLIT arrangement changed as a consequence.** Its headline lived in a ~400px side column,
which capped it at a size nobody could read and forced every SPLIT headline to be four words
long. The card and photo now sit side by side above the **same full-width lower third the NEWS
arrangement uses**, so the two arrangements differ only in their top zone and every headline
gets the full width. SPLIT's blue top banner went with it: once it inherited the shared footer,
it was carrying the offer twice.

**This defect is inherited, not new.** `build_ad4.py` and `build_ad5.py` — the Round 1 builders
this system came from — carry the same 11-25px type. Anything rebuilt from them needs the same
pass.

**Photos:** five of seven show a woman, matching a 93% female audience. Kade carries the two
authority ads (B3 and M1) where the claim is "in the women we assess".

Build: `_creative_build/build_round2.py` → `public/creative/round2/`.

## Pre-flight checklist

Unchanged from Round 1. Run it on every ad at the moment of upload.

- [ ] Image interrupts the pattern
- [ ] Headline creates a curiosity gap that only clicking closes
- [ ] A specific benefit is present so the algorithm can find the right people
- [ ] Lead-in copy earns the "see more" tap
- [ ] Readability grade 5 or below
- [ ] Under 2,200 characters
- [ ] Heavy line breaks, one idea per line
- [ ] Specific numbers, not vague claims
- [ ] Positive framing leads
- [ ] Link description is intriguing, not descriptive
- [ ] CTA button set to Learn More
- [ ] Creative, headline and copy all on one theme
- [ ] Does not look like an ad

## Numbers rule

**n=86 and n=88 are quotable exactly.** They are the strongest thing we own and the entire
positioning is "they cite, we measure."

**n=27 numbers are ratios only** — roughly half, about a third, one in twenty-five. The pattern
split is 13/10/3/1 out of 27 people and a decimal will move. Re-run
`scripts/state-of-the-data.ts` before upload and correct anything that has drifted.
