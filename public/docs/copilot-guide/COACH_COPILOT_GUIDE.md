## Start here

The co-pilot is a doctrine-trained mentor you talk with. It rides on every page of the dashboard as a floating bubble. On a **client's profile** it has read that client's file and answers grounded in it. On **any other page** it reads your whole roster and answers about the method in general.

Two rules run through everything it does:

- **You approve everything.** Nothing it drafts or edits reaches a client on its own. It is always a draft you review and publish.
- **Doctrine always wins.** It never overrides a client's readiness gates, phase order, injuries, or safety — not for your instruction, and not for your saved preferences.

Work through the lessons below once and you will run every client to the same standard.

---

## The lessons — one per capability

### 1. Explain a read *(on a client)*
Understand WHY the system landed a client where it did — the state, the phase, the gates — in plain terms you can act on.

- Open a client, tap the co-pilot bubble (bottom-right).
- Ask it to explain: *"Why did the synthesis put her in Remediation?"*, *"Walk me through his fat map."*

**Example:** *"Why is capacity Amber for him?"* → it cites his recovery and sleep answers and what that means for load.
**Won't do:** invent data. If the answer isn't in the file, it says so.

### 2. Pressure-test a call *(on a client)*
Have it argue against you, against the doctrine, so you don't talk yourself into progressing someone who isn't ready. This is the moat.

- State your intended call and ask it to challenge you.
- *"Doctrine says hold — talk me out of progressing him."* *"Should this be Restoration?"*

**Example:** *"I want to move her to Accumulation"* → it checks the readiness gates and tells you what has to be true first.
**Won't do:** rubber-stamp you. If the gates aren't clear, it holds the line.

### 3. Review a plan against doctrine *(on a client)*
Catch what the engine got wrong before you publish — the single habit that lets any coach reach the standard.

- After you generate a program or nutrition plan, ask: *"Review his program against the doctrine."*
- It reads the actual sessions and macros and flags what's off.

**Example:** it catches intensity sitting in a Restoration block, a calorie target that leaked into the training plan, or a meal count fighting an appetite-suppression med.
**Won't do:** change the plan itself. It reviews and advises; you make the change and publish.

### 4. Draft a program *(on a client)*
Get a full, doctrine-correct starting spec instead of a blank form.

- Tap **"＋ Draft a program"**.
- It proposes phase, goal, frequency, duration, training age, competency — each with a reason.
- Review, edit the block name, tap **"Generate this draft"**.

**Example:** for a stressed Remediation client it proposes a Restoration block, capacity goal, conservative volume — and tells you why.
**Won't do:** publish. It only ever saves a DRAFT. Nothing reaches the client until you publish it on the program page.

### 5. Refine a draft *(on a client)*
Change one thing without re-rolling the whole plan — swaps, tweaks, and structural edits.

- Tap **"✎ Refine program"** (or **"✎ Refine nutrition"**), then describe ONE change.
- *"Swap the barbell squat for a hip thrust."* *"Drop the bench to 3 sets."* *"Add a fourth day."* *"Drop Wednesday's carries."*
- It shows exactly what will change; tap Apply.

**Example:** *"Add a fourth day"* → it proposes a phase-appropriate session and adds it; the other three days stay byte-for-byte identical.
**Won't do:** make a change that breaks the phase, a gate, a stated injury, the frequency ceiling, or (nutrition) the protein anchor.

### 6. Draft & refine nutrition *(on a client)*
Everything above, for the nutrition plan — draft it, then edit foods and meals.

- Tap **"＋ Draft nutrition"** to propose the plan (entry state, protein anchor, carb demand, meals/day).
- Tap **"✎ Refine nutrition"** to change one thing: *"Swap the oats for berries."* *"Drop to 3 meals."*

**Example:** swap a food and it re-sums that meal's macros and the day's calorie band automatically — nothing desyncs.
**Won't do:** allow a change that would break the protein anchor, the calorie floor, or a dietary restriction (it flags it instead).

### 7. Read your roster + morning brief *(anywhere)*
See who needs you across ALL your clients without opening each profile.

- On any non-client page, the bubble shows a red badge = clients awaiting you.
- Open it and tap **"☀ Morning brief"**, or ask *"Who's drifting this week?"*, *"Who's due to progress?"*

**Example:** the brief groups your roster by urgency, names the doctrine reason for each, and the single next step.
**Won't do:** answer a deep single-client question from here — it points you to open that client's profile.

### 8. Set your preferences *(anywhere)*
Teach it how YOU like to coach so it applies that everywhere.

- Open the bubble on a non-client page → **"What I can help with"** → **"⚙ Set your coaching preferences"**.
- Write plain guidance: *"Favour 4-day splits when the gates allow."* *"Keep first blocks to 3 sets."* *"Prefer dairy-free swaps."*

**Example:** from then on, its drafts and advice lean your way — where the doctrine allows.
**Won't do:** let preferences override a client's readiness gates, phase, or safety. They are soft guidance only.

## Put it together — worked walk-throughs

**New client → their first block**

1. Open the client. Ask the co-pilot to explain their read so you understand the state and phase.
2. Tap **"＋ Draft a program"**. Sanity-check the proposed phase/goal against what it told you.
3. Generate. Then ask it: *"Review this program against the doctrine."*
4. Fix anything it flags via **"✎ Refine program"**, then publish.

**A client is drifting → triage it**

1. From any page, tap the co-pilot bubble and hit **"☀ Morning brief"**.
2. Find the client flagged as regressing; read the doctrine reason.
3. Open their profile, ask the co-pilot to pressure-test whether to hold or adjust.
4. If a plan change is needed, refine the draft — one change at a time.

## Keeping it honest — the flag loop

Every answer has a quiet **thumbs-down**. Use it whenever an answer is wrong or drifts from the doctrine. Flagged exchanges land in **Clients → Co-Pilot Review** to be checked and cleared. This is how the standard stays consistent as more coaches rely on it — catch drift before it spreads.

The best habit to build: after you generate any program or nutrition plan, ask the co-pilot to review it against the doctrine before you publish.
