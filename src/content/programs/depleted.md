# Body Recode - Self-Guided Program
## Body State: Depleted (Score 5–8/15)

---

## What This Program Is

Your scorecard identified your body as currently in a Depleted state. This means your system is operating under high cumulative stress load with reduced recovery capacity. Energy output is inconsistent, sleep is disrupted, and your body is prioritising stability over adaptation.

This is not a fitness problem. It is a state problem. Applying a standard training program to a Depleted body accelerates the problem rather than resolves it.

This 12-week program is built specifically for Depleted state. Every decision - training volume, intensity, nutrition - is designed around what your body can actually tolerate right now, not what a generic program assumes.

---

## What Depleted State Means for Your Prescription

| Signal | What it means | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| High cumulative stress load | System is already under pressure | Keep training stimulus low - no additional stress spikes |
| Reduced recovery capacity | Body cannot repair between sessions at normal rate | Extended rest periods, low session density |
| Energy inconsistency | Output unreliable day to day | Fixed structure, no intensity-dependent sessions |
| Sleep disruption | Restoration window compromised | Training timing matters - avoid high neural demand in evenings |

**Priority hierarchy for this state:** Recovery and Regulation (RRS) governs all decisions. Nothing overrides it. If recovery signals worsen at any point, reduce volume before anything else.

## Training Protocol

### Frequency
**3 sessions per week** - non-consecutive days (e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri)

A Depleted body cannot sustain 4–5 sessions. Three sessions allows adequate recovery spacing between exposures. Do not add sessions, even on weeks you feel better.

### Session Structure (all three sessions follow this structure)

| Block | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Movement preparation | 8–10 minutes. Joint mobility, activation. Not cardiovascular. |
| Block A - Primary | Compound movement | Main stimulus. One pattern only. |
| Block B - Secondary | Supporting movement | Reinforces primary without competing load |
| Block C - Accessory | Low-load accessory | Completion work. No fatigue cost. |
| Block D - Trunk | Trunk stability | Non-spinal-loading. Bracing, carries, anti-rotation. |

### Session Duration
45–55 minutes. No longer. Extended sessions compound stress without benefit in Depleted state.

### Volume - Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4)
| Block | Sets |
|---|---|
| Block A | 3 sets |
| Block B | 3 sets |
| Block C | 2 sets |
| Block D | 2 sets |
| **Total per session** | **10 sets** |

### Volume - Phase 2 (Weeks 5–8)
| Block | Sets |
|---|---|
| Block A | 4 sets |
| Block B | 3 sets |
| Block C | 3 sets |
| Block D | 2 sets |
| **Total per session** | **12 sets** |

### Volume - Phase 3 (Weeks 9–12)
| Block | Sets |
|---|---|
| Block A | 4 sets |
| Block B | 4 sets |
| Block C | 3 sets |
| Block D | 2–3 sets |
| **Total per session** | **13–14 sets** |

### Intensity
- Rep range: **8–12 reps** (hypertrophy/capacity range - no maximum strength work in Depleted state)
- RPE target: **RPE 5–6** in Phase 1. **RPE 6–7** in Phase 2–3.
- No training to failure. No AMRAP sets.
- If RPE exceeds target - reduce load immediately. Do not push through.

### Rest Periods
- Block A: 2.5–3 minutes
- Block B: 2 minutes
- Block C–D: 90 seconds

Longer rest periods than standard. In Depleted state, neural recovery between sets matters more than training density.

### Weekly Pattern Structure (3-day full body)

**Session 1**
- Block A: Squat pattern (goblet squat, leg press, or split squat)
- Block B: Horizontal pull (cable row, DB row)
- Block C: Horizontal push (DB press, push-up variation)
- Block D: Trunk stability (dead bug, pallof press)

**Session 2**
- Block A: Hinge pattern (Romanian deadlift, trap bar deadlift)
- Block B: Vertical pull (lat pulldown, assisted pull-up)
- Block C: Lunge / split stance (reverse lunge, step-up)
- Block D: Carry (farmer carry, suitcase carry)

**Session 3**
- Block A: Horizontal push (DB bench press, incline press)
- Block B: Squat or hinge variation (lighter than Session 1/2)
- Block C: Vertical pull or horizontal pull variation
- Block D: Anti-rotation / trunk (plank variation, bird dog)


### Progression Rules
- Do not increase load until you can complete all prescribed sets at the prescribed RPE with controlled technique.
- Increase load by the smallest available increment only.
- If recovery worsens across any week, reduce Block A to 3 sets and do not progress until recovery stabilises.
- One variable changes at a time. Never increase load and volume in the same week.

## Nutrition Protocol


### Principles for Depleted State

A Depleted body is under systemic stress. Nutrition in this state has one primary job: reduce the metabolic stress that is compounding the problem. This is not a fat loss phase. Attempting aggressive caloric restriction in Depleted state deepens the hole.

**What that means:**

1. **Do not eat in a deficit.** Maintenance calories or a small surplus. The body cannot recover from stress while also managing fuel scarcity.
2. **Prioritise protein.** Muscle protein synthesis is compromised in high-stress states. Adequate protein is the first nutritional priority.
3. **Do not skip meals.** Blood sugar volatility adds regulatory load to an already stressed system.
4. **Reduce processed food and alcohol.** Both elevate inflammatory load. In Depleted state, inflammation is already elevated.
5. **Eating window matters.** Avoid large meals close to bedtime - elevated insulin at night disrupts cortisol/melatonin cycling.

### Target Ranges

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Calories | Maintenance. No deficit. Estimate: bodyweight (kg) × 35–38 kcal. |
| Protein | 2.0–2.4g per kg of bodyweight daily |
| Carbohydrates | Moderate. Not restricted. Prioritise whole food sources - rice, potato, fruit. |
| Fat | Moderate. Prioritise saturated and monounsaturated sources - beef, eggs, butter, olive oil. |
| Meal frequency | 3–4 meals per day. Do not skip. |
| Eating window | First meal within 1 hour of waking. Last meal at least 2 hours before bed. |

### Priority Foods for Depleted State

These foods form the core of your intake in this state. They are nutrient-dense, low in inflammatory load, and support hormonal recovery.

| Priority | Food | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Beef (grass-fed where possible) | Complete amino acid profile, high iron and zinc - both depleted under chronic stress |
| 2 | Eggs (whole) | Highest bioavailable protein source, choline supports neural recovery |
| 3 | Salmon / fatty fish | Omega-3 reduces inflammatory load - elevated in Depleted state |
| 4 | Chicken thighs | High protein, higher fat content than breast - better for hormonal support |
| 5 | Liver (beef or chicken, 1–2x/week) | Highest nutrient density of any food - B vitamins, iron, copper, retinol |
| 6 | Full-fat dairy (if tolerated) | Caloric density without volume load - useful when appetite is suppressed |
| 7 | Butter / tallow for cooking | Stable cooking fats that don't add inflammatory load |

## Sleep and Recovery

Sleep is the primary recovery mechanism. In Depleted state, protecting sleep quality is more important than any training variable.

**Non-negotiable:**
- Consistent sleep and wake time - same time every day including weekends
- No screens 30 minutes before bed
- No training within 3 hours of sleep
- No caffeine after 12pm
- Dark, cool room

**Target:** 7.5–9 hours per night. If you are averaging less than 7 hours, raise this before anything else.

## What to Expect

**Weeks 1–4:** Sessions feel easy. That is correct. This is not insufficient stimulus - it is appropriate stimulus for your current state. Resisting the urge to do more is the discipline of this phase.

**Weeks 5–8:** Volume increases modestly. You should begin to notice more consistent energy and sleep quality. If you do not, do not advance to Phase 3.

**Weeks 9–12:** The body begins to shift toward Transitioning state. Sessions feel more manageable. Recovery becomes more predictable.

**At the end of 12 weeks:** A new scorecard will tell you whether you have moved into Transitioning or Ready state. Most people in Depleted state move to Transitioning within this window.

## Glossary

**RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)**
A scale from 1–10 that measures how hard a set feels. RPE 5 means you could do 5 more reps. RPE 7 means you could do 3 more. RPE 8 means you could do 2 more. RPE 10 is absolute maximum effort. This program uses RPE to guide load selection rather than prescribing a specific weight - because the right weight is different for every person and changes week to week.

**Sets**
A group of repetitions performed without stopping. "3 sets of 10" means you perform 10 reps, rest, then repeat that twice more.

**Reps (Repetitions)**
The number of times you perform a movement within one set.

**Rep Range**
A target window for reps per set (e.g. 8–12 reps). Choose a weight that makes the last rep of the range feel like the target RPE. If 12 reps feels too easy, increase the weight. If 8 reps feels too hard, reduce it.

**Compound Movement**
An exercise that works multiple muscle groups and joints at the same time. Examples: squat, deadlift, bench press, row. These are the primary training stimulus in each session.

**Accessory Movement**
A lower-priority exercise that supports the compound work without adding significant fatigue. Examples: bicep curl, lateral raise, leg curl.

**Trunk Stability**
Exercises that train the muscles that support and protect the spine - without loading the spine directly. Examples: plank, dead bug, pallof press, farmer carry. Not the same as "ab work."

**Axial Load**
Load that travels vertically through the spine. Exercises like back squats and overhead press carry high axial load. This program limits axial load in Depleted state to reduce nervous system stress.

**Deload**
A planned week of reduced training volume and intensity. Not a rest week - you still train. The purpose is to allow the accumulated fatigue from previous weeks to dissipate so the body can consolidate the gains made. Skipping a deload does not produce better results. It produces worse ones.

**Full Body Session**
A session that trains the whole body rather than focusing on one area. In a 3-day-per-week structure, full body sessions allow each muscle group to be trained more frequently - which is more effective than splits at lower frequencies.

**Progressive Overload**
The principle of gradually increasing the demand placed on the body over time. This is what drives adaptation. In this program, overload is applied through small load increases once performance and recovery targets are met - not by training harder regardless of readiness.

**Phase**
A distinct training block with a specific focus. Each phase in this program has a different volume, intensity, and intent. Moving through phases in order is important - skipping ahead does not accelerate results.

**Body State**
The classification your scorecard assigned you - Depleted, Transitioning, or Ready. It reflects how your body is currently handling stress, recovering from training, and managing energy output. It is not a permanent label. It changes as the conditions driving it change.
